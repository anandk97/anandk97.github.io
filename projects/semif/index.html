Semantic ifs from a 4B model

SemIf is an open-source project that answers small agent decisions (route this, retry that, does the evidence support X?) by reading the probability of each declared option straight from a language model's logits. There is no generated text to parse. I ran it on a laptop GPU, reproduced the published results, and scored it on four public benchmarks where the closed Jev service has published numbers.

How it works

The request carries the state, a question and the allowed options. The model reads it once, and the logits of the option tokens become a probability for each option.

01
StateUnstructured text: a ticket, a log, a passage.
02
Criteria and optionsDefined at runtime with the request, e.g. supported / contradicted / insufficient.
03
One forward passQwen3.5-4B in BF16. No token is sampled.
04
Typed probabilitiesSoftware branches on the argmax, or abstains when confidence is low.

What I ran

  • The upstream 144-row authored decision set, with per-family metrics and temperature calibration.
  • The 37-state × 21-criterion throughput workload (777 decisions) in three execution modes.
  • RACE-H, CommonsenseQA, MMLU-CF (100 items each, seed 42) and the exact 1,000 MMLU-Pro items that live Jev was scored on.

Where MMLU-Pro breaks

Accuracy by subject on the 1,000 items. Reading-heavy subjects hold up, while subjects that need multi-step calculation fall toward chance (about 0.10 with ten options). A single forward pass has no room to work a problem out.

Throughput

777 decisions: 37 long states with 21 criteria each. Reusing the state's key/value cache avoids re-reading it for every criterion. Bars show decisions per second.

RTX 4080 Laptop (my run)RTX 3090 (upstream)
On the laptop, serial reuse beat parallel branching, the reverse of the desktop result. The parallel mode batches 21 suffixes at once and peaked at 11.8 GB of the 12 GB card. Both reuse modes flipped 4 of 777 decisions relative to fresh scoring, from BF16 rounding (largest probability change 0.09).

Takeaways

  • For reading comprehension a 4B model is close to Jev: 0.92 against 0.95 on RACE-H, inside the interval.
  • The gap widens as tasks need knowledge and calculation, reaching 0.43 against 0.84 on MMLU-Pro.
  • Confidence is useful: on the authored set, the most confident quarter of decisions in every family had no errors.
  • Each decision took a median of 0.1–0.2 s on the laptop, with no output tokens.

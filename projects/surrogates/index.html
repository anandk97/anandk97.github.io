Models for dynamical systems

A map of ways to turn observations and physical knowledge into a useful model. Start with the question and the data; the right family follows from what you need to predict, explain, or preserve.

This is a working guide, not a leaderboard. Families overlap, and the descriptions below are starting points for choosing an approach.

01 / Equation discovery

Sparse equations

Find a small set of terms that explains how the state changes.

Model & task
SINDy and related sparse-library methods fit a compact differential equation for simulation or forecasting.
Data
Time-resolved states, with derivatives estimated from measurements or fitted through an integration scheme.
Assumptions
The dynamics are sufficiently observed and expressible using a small selection from a chosen candidate library.
Artifact
Terms and coefficients in an explicit equation; their meaning depends on the variables and library.
Limits
Noise, missing variables, correlated candidate terms and an incomplete library can make discovery unstable or misleading.

Existing project: The data-driven dynamics project describes identified equations, assimilation, and forecasts. Its equation-fitting approach is related to sparse discovery; it is not a claim that every result is a standard SINDy run.

02 / Compression & dynamics

Modal & reduced models

Represent a large state with a smaller set of coordinates.

Model & task
POD finds energetic spatial modes for compression; DMD fits approximately linear evolution between snapshots. Spectral submanifolds (SSMs) describe invariant manifolds and reduced dynamics associated with selected modes. A state-space model, also often abbreviated SSM, is a different concept: it describes state evolution and observations. A POD basis alone is not a dynamical model.
Data
Aligned field snapshots and temporal pairs for DMD; spectral-submanifold approaches may use governing equations or trajectories, depending on the method. State-space models use observations and may include inputs or a specified evolution law.
Assumptions
A useful low-dimensional representation exists; DMD relies on a linear evolution approximation, while spectral-submanifold methods require assumptions appropriate to the system and chosen modes.
Artifact
POD modes and amplitudes, DMD modes and eigenvalues, a manifold with reduced dynamics, or a state transition and observation model.
Limits
Truncation loses structure; linear dynamics can miss nonlinear transitions. A latent state is not automatically physically interpretable.

03 / Feature-based prediction

Classical machine learning

Learn a mapping from chosen features to a target.

Model & task
Regression, random forests, kernel methods and Gaussian processes can predict a response or the next state.
Data
Paired examples: parameters, summaries or lagged measurements alongside target values.
Assumptions
Features retain the relevant information and training examples cover the intended operating region.
Artifact
Coefficients, tree splits, feature effects or a predictive distribution, depending on the model; importance is not causality.
Limits
Feature engineering and extrapolation can be difficult; iterative forecasts may accumulate error and violate physical constraints.

04 / Functions to functions

Neural operators

Learn a mapping between fields or functions across a family of problems.

Model & task
FNO, DeepONet and Transolver learn solution maps, for example from initial conditions or material fields to a solution field. Transolver uses learned physics-aware slices and attention to exchange information between points.
Data
Many paired input and output fields, typically from simulations or measurements, plus coordinates or geometry when required.
Assumptions
Training cases represent the target distribution. Grid, geometry and resolution flexibility depend on the architecture and implementation.
Artifact
A learned field-to-field map; attention or spectral weights can be inspected but are not automatically physical explanations.
Limits
Data and compute costs, out-of-distribution inputs, conservation and rollout stability require explicit evaluation.

Existing runnable comparison: The neural operator comparison is the detailed existing project, with methods, experiments and links to code. Its results apply to the stated tasks and protocols, not every problem in this guide.

05 / Equations & discretization

Physics-based & FEM-derived

Begin with governing laws, boundary conditions and a discretization.

Model & task
A finite element method (FEM) solves a discretized boundary-value or evolution problem; reduced models derived from FEM project or approximate that system for cheaper repeated evaluation.
Data
Geometry, material parameters, forcing and boundary conditions; reduced models may also use simulation snapshots.
Assumptions
The governing laws and constitutive choices are adequate, and the mesh and numerical scheme resolve relevant behavior.
Artifact
Equations, mesh, stiffness and mass matrices, and potentially a reduced basis with projected operators.
Limits
Model-form error, uncertain parameters, mesh resolution and computational cost remain; reduction may fail outside its training regimes.

06 / Model plus correction

Hybrid corrections

Keep a simple model in the loop and learn what it misses.

Model & task
A residual model adds a learned correction to a baseline. A gated correction also learns when or how strongly to apply it; the gate need not correspond to a physical switch.
Data
Baseline predictions paired with observed or simulated targets across relevant states and regimes.
Assumptions
The baseline carries useful structure and the correction can be learned from representative residuals.
Artifact
The baseline, residual function and, if used, gate can be examined separately; their decomposition may not be unique.
Limits
Corrections can erase conservation, destabilize rollouts or extrapolate poorly. A gate is not a guarantee of safety.

07 / History to future

Time-window models

Use a sequence of recent observations to predict what comes next.

Model & task
LSTM and other sequence models map a history window to a forecast, using recurrent hidden state to summarize temporal context.
Data
Ordered sequences with consistent sampling, input windows and future targets; known controls can be included.
Assumptions
The history and inputs contain enough information about the state, and training sequences cover deployment conditions.
Artifact
A forecasting function and hidden state, rather than an explicit governing equation. The hidden state is not necessarily physically interpretable.
Limits
Window length, missing inputs, distribution shifts and accumulated rollout error matter; evaluation must separate one-step from multi-step forecasts.

Reading the evidence

What exists today

This page is a conceptual guide, not a runnable model or a claim that every family has been implemented here. Family-specific implementations and comparisons beyond the linked existing projects are planned / not established here; no new training or benchmark results are presented.

For Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (KS), keep validation data used for model selection separate from the public test data used for evaluation. A public test set consulted during development is not an untouched final test. The linked data-driven dynamics project describes its public-test results; the operator comparison has its own tasks and evaluation protocols. Do not treat scores across different tasks or splits as a direct ranking.

Before comparing models: specify the task, dataset and split, metric, prediction horizon, input information, training budget, and whether test data informed model selection.
